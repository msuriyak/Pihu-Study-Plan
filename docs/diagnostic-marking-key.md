## SECTION B — Physics rubric (21 marks)

**How to use this.** Each box below is worth 1 mark. Tick it if the *idea* appears anywhere in the answer, in any wording. You are not judging whether the physics is elegant — only whether the required element is present. When genuinely unsure, tick it; we are looking for a rough level, not a precise mark.

### B1 — Coulomb's law (3 marks)

- ☐ States the force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them
- ☐ Writes a formula of the form F = (1/4πε₀)·(q₁q₂/r²) — symbols may vary, but **q₁q₂ must be on top and r² on the bottom**
- ☐ Vector form present: includes a "hat" symbol (like r̂ or n̂), **or** states the force acts along the line joining the two charges
- ☐ States that in a medium the force is **reduced**, divided by K (or becomes 1/K times)

*Tick up to 4, award a maximum of 3.*

### B2 — Electric flux (3 marks)

- ☐ Defines flux as the number of field lines passing through a surface, **or** gives a formula like Φ = E·A or ∮E·dS
- ☐ SI unit stated as **N m²/C** or **V m** (either is correct)
- ☐ States the net flux through a surface enclosing a dipole is **zero**
- ☐ Justifies it: the total enclosed charge is zero (because +q and −q cancel), **or** mentions Gauss's law

*Tick up to 4, award a maximum of 3.*

### B3 — Field lines (3 marks)

- ☐ Diagram for a single positive charge: lines drawn radiating **outward** (arrows pointing away)
- ☐ Diagram for two positive charges: lines curving **away from each other**, with a visible gap or neutral point between them
- ☐ One correct property stated. Any of these counts: field lines never cross; they start on positive and end on negative charge; they are always perpendicular to a conductor's surface; their density indicates field strength; they never form closed loops

*Award up to 3.*

**Note separately: were the diagrams drawn at all?** Yes ☐ No ☐ — see the diagram flag below.

### B4 — Gauss's theorem, charged wire (5 marks)

- ☐ States Gauss's theorem, or writes Φ = q_enclosed/ε₀
- ☐ Diagram drawn showing a **cylinder** around the wire (this is the Gaussian surface)
- ☐ States that flux through the flat end faces is zero, or that E is perpendicular to the wire / parallel to the curved surface's normal
- ☐ Uses charge enclosed = λL (lambda times length)
- ☐ Final answer of the form **E = λ / (2πε₀r)**

*Award up to 5.*

### B5 — Torque on a dipole (5 marks)

- ☐ Diagram showing a dipole at an angle in a field, with forces marked on both charges
- ☐ States the two forces are equal and opposite, so the net force is zero
- ☐ Sets up torque as force × perpendicular distance, with a term like qE × 2a sin θ
- ☐ Final result **τ = pE sin θ** or in vector form **τ = p × E**
- ☐ Defines dipole moment (p = q × 2a, i.e. charge times separation) **and** states torque is maximum at θ = 90°

*Award up to 5.*

## PUTTING IT TOGETHER — what the numbers mean

### Section A → sizes the Class 11 backlog (the biggest unknown in the plan)

| Correct out of 20 | Reading | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 14+ | Class 11 is in good shape | Cut the Class 11 track to 3 hrs/week; move the time into JEE problem volume |
| 9–13 | Normal at this stage | Keep the 5 hrs/week as planned |
| 5–8 | Real backlog | Raise to 8 hrs/week; drop one chapter per fortnight from the Phase 1 intake table |
| Under 5 | Serious gap | Tell me — Phase 1 needs rebuilding around it |

**Then look at attempted vs. correct**, which matters as much as the total:
- **Attempted 17+, correct under 10** → over-attempting. She is guessing under pressure. This is a *strategy* problem, fixable in weeks with the three-pass discipline in Part 8 of the plan. Good news, in a sense.
- **Attempted under 12, most of them correct** → accurate but slow, or over-cautious. Also fixable, but with volume rather than strategy.
- **Physics much lower than Maths** → expected, and consistent with your description. Confirms the Class 11 track should be physics-weighted.
