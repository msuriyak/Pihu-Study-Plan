## PART 2 — Seven strategic decisions this plan is built on

**1. Session 1 is a rehearsal, not the attempt.**
The student sits it fully and seriously, but the plan does not distort December–January to peak for it. NTA takes the *better* of the two scores, so Session 1 has no downside risk. Its real value is a genuine percentile reading and exam-hall experience under real conditions, six weeks before it counts.

**2. The Class 11 syllabus is roughly 45% of the JEE paper and must be scheduled, not assumed.**
This plan reserves a permanent daily slot for it (~5 hrs/week, ~150 hours total). Size it against the diagnostic. Non-negotiable Class 11 blocks: Physics — Rotational Motion, SHM, Waves, Thermodynamics/KTG, Laws of Motion, Work-Energy; Chemistry — Mole Concept, Bonding, Equilibrium, Thermodynamics, Periodic Properties, and **GOC (which every Class 12 organic chapter sits on top of)**; Maths — Conics, Straight Lines & Circles, Sequences, Binomial, P&C, Complex Numbers, Trigonometry, Limits.

**3. Inorganic Chemistry is the highest marks-per-hour on the JEE paper — and it is exactly what the student dislikes.**
This is the single biggest available gain. Coordination Compounds, d-&f-Block and p-Block are ~30 JEE marks answerable in seconds with no calculation. The fix is not "memorise harder." It is: never present a bare list. Every fact gets hung on a reason (why Cu breaks the expected electronic configuration, why lanthanoid contraction produces the trend it does, why CFSE predicts the colour). Facts anchored to logic survive in a logical mind; naked facts fall out. Then spaced-repetition cards for the irreducible residue. Budget 20 min/day, every day, from Week 5 onward.

**4. Physics gets a split personality.**
Board physics and JEE physics are almost different subjects for this student. Board physics = extensive theory, articulated in writing, with derivations and diagrams. JEE physics = numericals under time pressure. The plan handles them as separate layers within each chapter rather than hoping one transfers to the other. The student is described as "good at numericals but not proficient" — that gap closes with volume, not with more theory.

**5. Repetition is scheduled, not hoped for.**
The student needs more than one read. Rather than a full second pass of everything (which won't fit), every chapter enters a **spaced revision queue** on completion and is revisited at Day +7, Day +21 and Day +60. Three touches, each shorter than the last, spread across the plan. This is more effective than two heavy reads and costs less time.

**6. Boards get primacy on content; JEE gets primacy on speed.**
Where the syllabi overlap (which is most of Maths and much of Physics), one deep pass serves both. Where they diverge, board needs win before mid-February and JEE needs win after.

**7. Testing is scheduled workload, not something done "if there's time."**
Exam practice is budgeted in hours from Week 1 and grows to consume roughly half the week by January. Two rules govern it. First, **review time is at least equal to test time** — a three-hour mock is a six-hour commitment, and an unreviewed mock is worse than no mock, because it burns a paper and teaches nothing. Second, **the exam is a separate skill from the syllabus.** Question selection, attempt order, when to abandon a question, and how to gamble under negative marking are worth 30–50 marks on the JEE paper to a student who knows the content equally well. Since you've told me maximisation matters more than depth, this is where a large part of the gain lives. See Part 8.

## PART 4 — The chapter cycle (the engine of this plan)

Every chapter goes through the same five layers. This is what makes requirements 5–8 automatic rather than something to remember.

**L1 — Concept intake (~25% of chapter time)**
NCERT read + coaching/online lecture. Aim for understanding, not mastery. Do *not* linger here — the second and third passes are already scheduled.

**L2 — Board layer (~25%)**
NCERT in-text + exercise questions, all of them. Then chapter-wise board PYQs (last 10 years). Critically: **write out at least two 3-mark and one 5-mark answer longhand, on paper, timed.** For Physics, include the derivation with a labelled diagram. Board marks are lost to presentation, not knowledge.

**L3 — Recall layer (~10%, memory-heavy chapters only)**
Applies to: all Inorganic, Biomolecules, Amines/Alcohols name reactions, Physics definitions & SI units, PE, English value points.
Output = one A4 "recall sheet" (the student writes it, not copies it) + spaced-repetition cards.

**L4 — JEE layer (~30%)**
Chapter-wise JEE PYQs 2019–2026, then a **timed 15-question sprint** at exam pace. Log every error in the error journal with a one-line cause tag: *concept / silly / speed / didn't know*. That tag distribution is the most useful diagnostic in the whole plan.

**L5 — Spaced revisits (~10%)**
Chapter enters the queue. Revisit at **Day +7** (30 min: recall sheet + 10 mixed questions), **Day +21** (20 min: errors only + 10 questions), **Day +60** (15 min: recall sheet + 5 questions). Log the three dates the moment a chapter is finished.

**Estimated chapter cost:** Heavy 12–14 hrs · Medium 8–10 hrs · Light 5–6 hrs.

### Phase 4 — Board lockdown (25 Jan – 16 Feb 2027, ~3 weeks)

**100% boards. Zero JEE.** This is the hardest instruction in the plan and the most important. The JEE syllabus is already loaded; it will keep for six weeks. Board marks are earned in exactly this window.

- Full board syllabus revision, subject by subject, using recall sheets and PYQs only.
- **One full board paper every alternate day, longhand, timed** — roughly 10 papers across the phase. Rotate: Physics, Chemistry, Maths, English, PE, then repeat. Draw from CBSE sample papers first, then 2024–2026 actual papers. Self-mark every one against the official marking scheme and record the score; a rising curve across three weeks is the best possible confidence input going into 17 February.
- The alternate days are review days — rework every question that lost marks, and specifically log *why*: missed value point, missing diagram, ran out of time, didn't know.
- Daily longhand writing practice — this is where "articulation needs effort" gets fixed. Physics theory answers and English literature answers, timed, marked against the marking scheme's value points.
- **Physical Education gets a concentrated block here** — it is largely recall-based and retains best when learned close to the exam. Budget 12–15 hrs total across this phase.
- English: all 19 prose/poem pieces revised, plus the writing section (notice, letter, article, report) which is 40%+ of the paper and often neglected.

### Phase 5 — Board exam window (17 Feb – late Mar 2027)

- Live off the date sheet. The gap between papers is the study time for the next paper — nothing else.

### Phase 6 — JEE Session 2 sprint (last board paper → early Apr 2027)

Depending on the date sheet, this is 10–20 days. Treat it as the highest-intensity window of the entire plan — the student will be exam-fit, board content will be at peak sharpness, and there is nothing else competing.

### The review protocol — the part most students skip

A mock is not the learning event. The review is. Run it in this exact order, and never before the next morning (overnight consolidation genuinely helps, and reviewing while demoralised teaches nothing):

1. **Re-attempt cold.** Every question that was wrong, skipped or flagged — redo it untimed, *before opening any solution*. Many will now come out. Those were never knowledge failures; they were time or nerve failures, and they need a completely different fix.
2. **Then solutions.** Only for what survived step 1.
3. **Tag every error** — *concept / silly / speed / didn't know / mis-gamble*.
4. **Extract to the error journal**, one line each.
5. **Record the four numbers** (below).

### The four numbers to track after every mock

Plot these across the year. A student who likes logic will engage with a moving graph far more than with exhortation.

- **Accuracy %** — correct ÷ attempted. Should sit around 85%+ under negative marking. Below 75% means over-attempting, not under-knowing.
- **Attempt %** — attempted ÷ total. The dial that trades against accuracy.
- **Time per correct answer** — total time ÷ correct answers. The single best measure of exam efficiency.
- **Leakage** — marks lost to *silly / speed / mis-gamble* tags, i.e. everything that was known but not scored. For most students this is 20–40 marks. **This is the cheapest score improvement available anywhere in this plan**, and it is invisible unless measured.

### JEE attempt strategy — drill this from Phase 2

Worth 30–50 marks to a student with identical knowledge. It is a skill; it needs its own reps.

**Order.** Chemistry first, always — it is the fastest section and mostly recall, so it banks certain marks early and settles nerves. Target 30–35 min. Then Maths (this student's strength — a strong second section builds momentum). Physics last.

**Three-pass sweep within each section.**
- *Pass 1:* attempt only what you can see the solution path to within about 15 seconds. Everything else gets flagged and abandoned instantly. No exceptions, no "just one more minute."
- *Pass 2:* return to flagged questions, up to 3 min each. Abandon again at 3 min.
- *Pass 3 (final ~20 min):* gambling calculus.

**The gambling calculus.** JEE Main scores +4 / −1. On a 4-option MCQ, a blind guess has expected value +0.25 — marginally positive. Eliminate even one option and it rises to about +0.67. So: **never leave an MCQ blank if a single option can be ruled out.** Section B numerical questions have carried negative marking since 2025, so a blind numerical guess is pure expected loss — leave those blank. *Verify both against the 2027 information bulletin when it releases in Oct–Nov 2026; the marking scheme has changed twice in recent years.*

**Drill it explicitly.** For three mocks in Phase 2, the goal is not the score — it is executing the sweep without deviation. Have the student note, after each mock, how many times they broke the 15-second rule. That number should fall to zero.

### Board attempt strategy — drill this in Phase 4

- **Use the 15-minute reading time deliberately:** mark the questions to do first, and mentally outline the two 5-markers. Don't just read passively.
- **Answer in the marker's currency.** Value points, not prose. Underline keywords. Diagram for every question that can take one, labelled, even where not explicitly asked. A 5-mark Physics derivation without a diagram loses a mark almost every time.
- **Length discipline:** roughly 1 point per mark, plus a diagram. Over-writing costs time, never marks.
- **Never leave blanks.** There is no negative marking. A partially remembered definition scores; a blank does not.
- For an avid reader, the failure mode in English is a fluent, well-written answer that scores 3/5 because it missed two required value points. Self-marking against the official scheme is what surfaces this.

## PART 9 — Subject playbooks

**Maths.** The strength — protect it and monetise it. The gap is JEE speed, not understanding. Every chapter's L4 sprint should be timed at ~2 min/question and the time recorded. Track speed as a number over the weeks; a logical student responds well to seeing a curve move. From Phase 2, add one 25-question mixed Maths set weekly drawing from all chapters covered so far — mixed retrieval is what board and JEE papers actually demand.

**Physics.** Two separate deliverables per chapter. *Board:* a written derivation bank — every derivation in the syllabus, written out once longhand with diagram, collected in one book. Roughly 25 derivations total, and they recur year after year. *JEE:* volume of numericals. The "not very proficient" gap is closed by problem count, not by more theory. Target 60+ JEE questions per heavy chapter.

**Chemistry.** The physical chemistry chapters (Solutions, Electrochemistry, Kinetics) will come easily — treat them as numerical practice like Physics. Organic is pattern recognition, not memory: build a single reaction map per chapter showing what converts to what, and the "exceptions" mostly dissolve into mechanism. Inorganic is the deliberate campaign described in Part 2, decision 3 — 20 minutes daily from Week 5, reason-scaffolded, never as bare lists.

**English.** An avid reader will have no comprehension problem and may still underperform, because CBSE literature marking rewards *value points*, not fluency. Method: for each piece, read once for pleasure, then extract 6–8 value points that examiners look for, then write one 120-word answer under time and self-mark against the official marking scheme. The pattern to watch for is a beautifully written answer scoring 3/5 because it missed two required points. Also do not neglect the writing section — notice, letter, article, report are formula-driven, high-scoring, and quickly learned.

**Physical Education.** Low effort, high return, and best deferred. Light touch (45 min/week in the Friday rotating slot) through Phase 1 and 2, then a concentrated 12–15 hour block in Phase 4. Confirm which units your syllabus covers so this can be scheduled precisely.

### The four artefacts

1. **Error journal.** Every wrong answer, one line, with a cause tag: *concept / silly / speed / didn't know*. Reviewed every Sunday. The tag distribution tells you what to fix — a "silly" majority needs a different intervention from a "concept" majority.
2. **Spaced-revision queue.** A dated list. When a chapter finishes, immediately write its Day+7, Day+21 and Day+60 dates. Each day's queue is worked in the morning warm-up slot.
3. **Flashcard deck.** Inorganic-weighted, plus Physics definitions/SI units, name reactions, and English value points.
4. **Test log.** One row per test: date, type, paper source, score, the four numbers, and a "reviewed ✓" box. The reviewed box is the whole point — it makes an unreviewed backlog impossible to ignore.

### Weekly review ritual (60 min, Sunday evening)

- Chapters completed vs. planned — if behind, what gets compressed, not what gets dropped

- Error journal tag distribution for the week

- Spaced-revision queue check — any chapter overdue?

## PART 11 — Risks and open items

**1. CBSE exam registration — action this in Week 0.**
For the 2026 cycle, the Class 12 private candidate form opened 9 Sept 2025 and closed 30 Sept 2025 (late fee to 11 Oct). The 2027 equivalent falls in **September 2026 — about six weeks from now.** Two things to verify immediately:
