# THE GUARDIAN'S OPERATING MANUAL

*How to run this plan for eight months without knowing the subjects*

---

## PART 1 — Your actual job

You are not the teacher. The coaching, the online course and the books teach the content. Trying to teach it yourself is the one thing that will reliably go wrong: you will be wrong occasionally, she will notice, and the trust you need for the rest of this will erode.

**Your job is to audit the process, not the physics.** That turns out to be the more important job, because almost every student who underperforms does so for process reasons — tests not reviewed, revision skipped, errors never analysed — and almost none of those reasons require subject knowledge to spot.

Three specific functions:

1. **Timekeeper.** You start and stop the clock on tests. She cannot invigilate herself.
2. **Auditor.** You verify that the artefacts exist — the recall sheets, the reviewed test log, the error journal. Not that they are *correct*; that they are *there*.
3. **Regulator.** You protect the rest days and the sleep, and you absorb the emotional volatility around bad scores so she doesn't have to manage it alone.

**The one thing to internalise:** you can tell whether someone understands something without understanding it yourself. You do this constantly in ordinary life. Part 3 turns it into a method.

---

## PART 2 — How to run a test

### Before

- Fix the slot in advance and treat it as immovable. Full JEE mocks start at **9:00 AM** — the same time as her exam shift, so the body learns to peak then.
- Phone in another room. Yours too.
- Blank rough sheets, pen, water. Nothing else on the desk.
- For computer-based mocks, the full three hours in one sitting with no pausing. **Pausing a mock is the single most common way students fool themselves.**

### During

Your only job is timekeeping. Do not hover, do not enter the room, do not ask how it's going. Call out the halfway point and the last fifteen minutes for board papers; for JEE mocks the interface does it.

If she wants to stop early, let her — but note the time and ask about it the next day, not that day.

### After

- **No marking on the day** for anything longer than an hour. Overnight matters: reviewing while demoralised teaches nothing and poisons the next test.
- **She marks, not you** (except the Week 0 diagnostic, which you mark because a baseline should be independent). Self-marking against an official scheme is itself one of the highest-value learning activities in the plan — it is how she internalises what a marker actually rewards. Do not take it away from her.
- Your role is to check the marking *happened* and to record the numbers in the test log.

### The three questions to ask after every test

These require zero subject knowledge and get you almost everything you need:

1. **"How many did you get wrong that you knew?"** — this is the leakage number, the cheapest marks in the whole plan.
2. **"Which one question cost you the most time?"** — surfaces the abandon-discipline problem.
3. **"What will you do differently in the next one?"** — if the answer is "study harder," push back. It should be specific: *I'll do Chemistry first*, *I'll drop anything I can't start in 15 seconds*, *I'll draw the diagram before writing*.

If the third answer is vague three tests running, the review protocol isn't being followed. That's a real finding and you found it without knowing any physics.

## PART 3 — The teach-back: your best instrument

**Once a week, Sunday, 5 minutes. She explains one chapter to you as if you'd never heard of it.**

This is the most powerful thing in this manual. You cannot judge whether her answer to a Gauss's law problem is right — but you can absolutely judge whether an explanation is fluent and structured or halting and hedged. That judgement is reliable, it requires no expertise, and it correlates strongly with actual mastery. Genuine understanding compresses; half-understanding rambles.

**Score it out of 4. Tick what's true:**

- ☐ **Started with the point.** Opened with what the chapter is *about* or *for*, rather than diving into the first formula on page one.
- ☐ **Didn't need to look anything up.** No glancing at notes, no "hang on, let me check."
- ☐ **Used an example or analogy** of her own, not one read out of a book.
- ☐ **Answered a follow-up question** without visible panic. Ask a genuinely naive one — *"why does that matter?"*, *"what happens if you double that?"*, *"where would you see this in real life?"*

**3–4:** the chapter is solid. **2:** shaky, schedule an extra revision pass. **0–1:** she has read it, not learned it — that chapter goes back into the queue this week.

Two rules. Choose the chapter yourself, from anything covered in the last month, and tell her only at the start — a teach-back she prepared for measures preparation, not understanding. And when she stalls, stay silent and wait. The stall *is* the data. Rescuing her destroys the instrument.

## PART 4 — The weekly task pack

This is how you gauge progress without touching the content. Every Sunday she hands over five things. All five are countable or inspectable by anyone.

| # | Deliverable | What you check | Takes you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Recall sheets** for chapters finished this week | One A4 per chapter, **handwritten**, one side only. That it exists and is in her handwriting — not printed, not photographed from a book | 1 min |
| 2 | **Test log rows** for every test scheduled that week | Each row filled, and critically the **"reviewed ✓" box ticked** | 2 min |
| 3 | **Error journal entries** | That entries exist, and that each has a tag: *concept / silly / speed / didn't know / mis-gamble*. Count how many of each | 3 min |
| 4 | **One teach-back** | Score it out of 4 as in Part 3 | 5 min |
| 5 | **One self-marked written answer** (board style) with the value points ticked off in her own hand | That the ticking happened against an official marking scheme — not a self-assessment of "yeah that's fine" | 2 min |

Fifteen minutes a week. That's the whole audit.

**What the error tags tell you** — this is the most informative number you'll have, and it needs no subject knowledge at all:

| Dominant tag | What it means | What to change |
|---|---|---|
| Mostly *silly* | Knowledge is fine, execution is leaking | Attempt strategy and slowing down on the first pass. Fastest available gain |
| Mostly *speed* | Knows it, can't do it in time | More timed sprints, fewer new chapters |
| Mostly *concept* | Genuine content gaps | That chapter needs another pass — flag it to me |
| Mostly *didn't know* | Syllabus coverage gap, not a skill gap | Check it against the Phase 1 schedule; something has been skipped |

A shift from *concept* toward *silly* over the months is exactly what progress looks like. Point it out to her when it happens.

## PART 5 — How to talk about it

**Never ask "did you study today?"** It invites a yes and teaches nothing. Ask about artefacts instead: *"can I see this week's test log?"* Objects are harder to fudge than assurances, and the question carries no accusation.

**Audit weekly, not daily.** Daily monitoring produces resentment and gaming — she'll optimise for looking busy rather than learning. Weekly is frequent enough that nothing drifts far.

**Praise process, never scores.** "You reviewed every mock this week" is a sentence about something she controls. "You got 210" is a sentence about the paper's difficulty as much as her. Students praised for scores get frightened of hard papers; students praised for process seek them out. Over eight months this compounds enormously.

**Never react to one number.** Mock scores drop when papers get harder and when the syllabus broadens — both of which are supposed to happen. **React only to a three-test trend.** Say this out loud to her now, before the first bad score, so it doesn't sound like consolation invented after the fact.

**Pre-agree what happens after JEE Session 1 in January: nothing.** The score will very likely disappoint — it's a rehearsal taken mid-preparation, and NTA counts only the better of the two attempts. Agree the response *today*, in writing if it helps: three days off, one analysis session, back to the plan. A January score that gets treated as a verdict can cost more in February than the paper was ever worth.

**Rest is a scheduled item, not a reward for output.** The half-day off, the post-exam break, the sleep — those are in the plan for the same reason the mocks are. Do not let her trade them away on a bad week. She will try.

**When she's struggling and you can't help with the content** — and this will happen — the honest response is the good one: *"I can't help with the physics, but I can help you work out who can. Shall we take this to the coaching doubt session, or shall I ask about it?"* Offering process help you can actually deliver builds more trust than pretending to competence you don't have.

**One thing worth saying explicitly, once:** she is doing something hard, and being homeschooled means doing it without the ambient company of thirty other people doing the same thing. That isolation is a real cost and it's worth naming rather than leaving her to assume everyone finds this easier. If there's any way to get her into a peer group — a coaching batch that meets, a study group, even an online one — take it. It's worth more than another book.

## PART 6 — Books

The most common mistake here is buying too much. Every extra book is a decision she has to make each day about which one to open, and unfinished books breed guilt. **One core book per purpose, finished, beats five started.** The list below is deliberately short. Buy the "start now" column in Week 0 and nothing else until Phase 2.

### Mathematics

| Purpose | Book | When |
|---|---|---|
| Board — foundation | **NCERT Class 12 Maths** Parts 1 & 2 | Start now |
| Board — depth | **NCERT Exemplar Problems, Class 12 Maths** | Start now |
| Board — PYQ | **Oswaal CBSE Question Bank, Class 12 Mathematics** (chapter-wise past papers + sample papers) | Start now |
| JEE — practice | **Cengage Mathematics (G. Tewani)** — buy one volume at a time as the chapter comes up, not the set | Start now (Calculus volume) |
| JEE — PYQ | **Arihant 40 Years' JEE Main Chapterwise Mathematics** (or the MTG equivalent) | Start now |

*Cengage suits a logical student who likes the subject. If it feels heavy, Arihant's "Skills in Mathematics" series by Amit M. Agarwal is the gentler alternative — but pick one and stay with it.*

### Physics

| Purpose | Book | When |
|---|---|---|
| Board — foundation | **NCERT Class 12 Physics** Parts 1 & 2. Non-negotiable; board questions come almost verbatim from it | Start now |
| Board — depth + PYQ | **Oswaal CBSE Question Bank, Class 12 Physics** | Start now |
| JEE — concepts | **H. C. Verma, Concepts of Physics Vol. 2** — for understanding, worked examples, and the short-answer sections | Start now |
| JEE — numerical volume | **D. C. Pandey, Understanding Physics (Arihant)** — the *Electricity & Magnetism* and *Optics & Modern Physics* volumes. This is where the "needs practice" gap actually closes | Start now |
| JEE — PYQ | **Arihant 40 Years' JEE Main Chapterwise Physics** | Start now |

**Do not buy Irodov.** It is aimed well above JEE Main, and for a Mains-focused candidate it is a time sink that produces frustration rather than marks. If someone recommends it, that recommendation is calibrated for a different exam.

### Chemistry

| Purpose | Book | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Inorganic — everything** | **NCERT Class 12 Chemistry**, read line by line. JEE Main inorganic questions are notoriously lifted straight from NCERT sentences. This is the highest return per hour of any book on this list | Start now |
| Inorganic — reference | **J. D. Lee, Concise Inorganic Chemistry** (adapted Indian edition, Sudarshan Guha) — reference only, for d/f-block and coordination. Not read cover to cover | Phase 2 |
| Physical — numericals | **N. Awasthi, Problems in Physical Chemistry (Balaji)** — **do Level 1 and Level 2 only, skip Level 3.** Level 3 is JEE Advanced material | Start now |
| Organic | **M. S. Chouhan, Advanced Problems in Organic Chemistry (Balaji)** — again, the earlier exercises only | Phase 1, from Week 9 |
| Board — PYQ | **Oswaal CBSE Question Bank, Class 12 Chemistry** | Start now |

*The instruction to skip the hardest exercises is deliberate and worth repeating to her. Both Awasthi and Chouhan are written for JEE Advanced. Working the Level 3 problems will cost hours and buy almost nothing on a Mains paper.*

### English Core

| Purpose | Book |
|---|---|
| Texts | **NCERT Flamingo** and **NCERT Vistas** — the prescribed books themselves |
| Practice + PYQ | **Oswaal** or **Educart CBSE Question Bank, Class 12 English Core** |
| Essential and free | **CBSE official sample papers *and marking schemes*** from cbseacademic.nic.in. The marking scheme is the more valuable document — it shows exactly which points earn marks |

### Physical Education

| Purpose | Book |
|---|---|
| Core | **Saraswati, Health and Physical Education, Class 12** (the standard text most schools use) |
| PYQ | **Oswaal CBSE Question Bank, Class 12 Physical Education** |

### Free resources worth having

- **cbseacademic.nic.in** — sample papers, marking schemes, the official syllabus document. All free, all authoritative, and the marking schemes are what everything in Part 4 is checked against.
- **NTA Abhyas app** — official NTA practice, on the real exam interface. Useful specifically because the *interface* matters.
- **jeemain.nta.nic.in** — past papers and answer keys released after each session.

### Approximate spend

The "start now" column is roughly ₹8,000–12,000. Everything else is free or comes later. If budget is a constraint, the true irreducible core is: **all five NCERTs, one Oswaal question bank per subject, and one chapter-wise JEE PYQ book.** That set alone, worked thoroughly, is enough for a strong board result and a competitive Mains score.

## PART 7 — Red flags

Things you can spot without knowing any subject. Any of these means something is wrong; the first three mean it's wrong *now*.

1. **Unreviewed tests accumulating.** Two unticked "reviewed" boxes in the log is a warning; three is a stop-and-fix. This is the earliest and most reliable signal that the plan is slipping.
2. **Recall sheets that are copied, not written.** Printed, photographed, or transcribed sheets mean the retrieval step is being skipped — and retrieval is the entire mechanism by which the second read works.
3. **Error journal entries with no tags, or all tagged the same.** Means the review is being performed rather than done.
4. **Teach-back score stuck at 1–2 for three weeks.** Content is being covered, not learned. Tell me which chapters and we'll change the approach.
5. **Sleep dropping below seven hours on a regular basis.** Below that, retention falls faster than the extra hours add. This is not a moral point, it's an arithmetic one.
6. **She stops complaining.** Counterintuitive, but a student who has gone quiet and compliant is often one who has given up on the plan and is going through the motions. Friction is usually a sign of engagement.

## A closing note

Eight months is a long time and the middle of it — roughly October to December — is where most plans quietly die. Not dramatically; they just stop being followed. The single practice that prevents this is the Sunday audit, done every week without exception, in the same fifteen minutes, whether or not it seems necessary that week. Everything else in this manual is downstream of that habit.

You've said you're not an educator. For this role, that's genuinely not the limitation it feels like. The things that decide the outcome here — consistency, honest measurement, protected rest, and someone paying steady attention — are not subject expertise. They're the things you're already positioned to provide.
